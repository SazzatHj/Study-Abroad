## 🌸 Overview

The **MEXT Scholarship** is a fully funded scholarship offered by the **Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) of Japan**. It is open to international students who want to study at Japanese universities and colleges.

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Provider** | Government of Japan (MEXT) |
| **Coverage** | Tuition waiver, monthly allowance, round-trip airfare |
| **Levels** | Undergraduate, Master's, PhD, Research Student, KOSEN, Specialized Training College |
| **Main routes** | Embassy Recommendation, University Recommendation |
| **Language** | English-taught and Japanese-taught programs are available |
| **Apply to** | The Japanese Embassy/Consulate in your country (do **not** send documents directly to MEXT) |

---

## 🎓 Types of MEXT Scholarship

| Category | Level | Typical duration | Notes |
|----------|-------|------------------|-------|
| **Undergraduate Students** | Bachelor's | About 5 years (7 for medicine, dentistry, veterinary medicine, pharmacy) | Includes a 1-year intensive Japanese language course |
| **Research Students** | Master's, PhD, or non-degree research | Starts April/Sept/Oct, runs up to about 2 years for the scholarship period | Most popular route for graduates; Japanese Embassy screening first |
| **College of Technology (KOSEN)** | 3rd-year entry | About 4 years | For high-school graduates in technical fields |
| **Specialized Training College** | Vocational diploma | About 4 years | Includes Japanese language training |
| **Teacher Training** | Teachers | About 1.5 years | Limited to certain countries and applicants |
| **Young Leaders Program (YLP)** | Master's | About 2 years | Government officials in selected countries |

> Each country may be offered only some of these categories. Check which ones your Embassy offers.

---

## 🏛️ Embassy vs University Recommendation

| | **Embassy Recommendation** | **University Recommendation** |
|---|---|---|
| **Who selects first** | Japanese Embassy/Consulate in your country | The Japanese university itself |
| **Where you apply** | Embassy (then contact universities for acceptance) | Directly to the university's program |
| **Competition** | Very high, since it is national | Depends on university and program |
| **Programs** | Wide range of fields | Only specific partner or designated programs |
| **Best for** | Applicants who can pass national screening | Applicants targeting a particular university program |

> Both routes are final-approved by MEXT.

## 💴 Benefits

Amounts are approximate and change by year. Confirm in the current guidelines.

| Benefit | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Tuition** | Entrance fee, tuition, and examination fees are covered |
| **Monthly allowance** | About ¥117,000 (undergraduate), about ¥143,000–¥145,000 (Master's/PhD/Research Student) |
| **Regional supplement** | Extra amount may apply for students in certain higher-cost areas |
| **Airfare** | Economy-class ticket to Japan at the start and back at the end of the scholarship |
| **Other** | Possible support for approved research-related expenses, depending on the category |

## ✅ General Eligibility

These are common conditions. Your Embassy's guidelines are the final authority.

- **Nationality:** Citizen of a country that has diplomatic relations with Japan. Dual nationals including Japanese nationality are generally not eligible.
- **Age:** Strict upper limit by category. For Research Students, applicants are generally **under 35 years old** at the time of arrival (check the exact birth-date cutoff in the guidelines).
- **Academic background:**
  - Undergraduate: completed 12 years of school education
  - Research Student: bachelor's degree (for Master's) or master's degree (for PhD) or equivalent
- **Academic performance:** Strong academic record, as shown by transcripts and the Embassy's selection process.
- **Health:** Good physical and mental health to study in Japan; a Certificate of Health is required.
- **Arrival in Japan:** Must be able to arrive in Japan by the date set by MEXT or the university.
- **No double scholarship:** Cannot receive another scholarship that has not been approved for concurrent receipt.
- **Previous MEXT recipients:** Generally not eligible again, with limited exceptions listed in the guidelines.
- **Japanese language:** Required for some programs; others are fully English-taught.

## 📅 Yearly Timeline

Typical cycle for **Research Students (Embassy Recommendation)**. Dates differ by country.

| Month | What happens |
|-------|--------------|
| **Mar–Apr** | Embassy announces recruitment and releases guidelines |
| **Apr–May** | Application submission period at the Embassy (deadlines vary by country) |
| **May–Jul** | First screening: document review, written exams, interview |
| **Jul–Aug** | First screening results announced |
| **Aug–Oct** | Passed applicants obtain acceptance or placement from universities |
| **Oct–Dec** | MEXT second screening and universities' placement |
| **Dec–Mar** | Final results; preparation for departure |
| **Apr or Sep/Oct (next year)** | Arrival in Japan and start of the scholarship |

> For intake in 2028, recruitment is expected to open around **spring 2027**. Start preparing months earlier.

## 🪜 Step-by-Step Application (Embassy Recommendation)

1. **Check the Embassy website** of Japan in your country (for Bangladeshi applicants: the Embassy of Japan in Dhaka) for the current announcement.
2. **Read the official Application Guidelines** for your category line by line.
3. **Confirm eligibility:** age, degree, nationality, and field.
4. **Prepare documents** (see checklist below), including transcripts, certificates, recommendation letter, health certificate, and study/research plan.
5. **Submit your application to the Embassy** before the deadline, exactly as instructed (post, in person, or other method specified).
6. **Prepare for the first screening:** written exams (English, Japanese if required, and subject exams) and interview.
7. **Wait for the first-screening result** from the Embassy.
8. **Contact universities** if required by your category and obtain placement or acceptance as instructed.
9. **Wait for MEXT's final decision.**
10. **Complete pre-departure procedures** (visa, travel, accommodation) and arrive in Japan on schedule.

> ❗ Never send application documents directly to MEXT in Japan.

## 📂 Documents Checklist

Exact forms depend on category and year. Use this as a planning list.

- [ ] Application form (official MEXT form for the year)
- [ ] Placement preference form (if applicable)
- [ ] Field of study / research plan
- [ ] Academic transcripts (all institutions)
- [ ] Certificate of graduation (or expected graduation)
- [ ] Certificate of Health, signed by a licensed doctor
- [ ] Recommendation letter from your university president, dean, or professor (and/or employer if required)
- [ ] Abstract of thesis (for those who have written one)
- [ ] Certificate of language proficiency (English and/or Japanese, if required)
- [ ] Photographs (as specified)
- [ ] Copy of passport or ID (if requested)
- [ ] Any additional document requested by the Embassy

## 📝 Screening: Exams and Interview

| Stage | What to expect |
|-------|----------------|
| **Document screening** | Embassy checks eligibility and quality of your forms |
| **Written exams** | Usually English, and Japanese language if your program requires it; subject exams (math, science, or others depending on your field) |
| **Interview** | Questions about your goals, research plan, academic background, and why Japan |

**Preparation tips**
- Past exam questions are published on the official Study in Japan website, so practice with them.
- Prepare to explain your research plan clearly and simply.
- Practice common interview questions: *Why Japan? Why this field? What will you do after returning?*

## 🔬 Choosing a University and Professor

For Research Student applicants, a good match with a professor strengthens your application.

1. List universities that offer your field in English or Japanese.
2. Read professors' profiles and recent publications.
3. Write a short, polite email to introduce yourself and your research interest.
4. Attach your CV and a short research proposal.
5. Keep a record of replies in your tracker.

**Sample email outline**
```
Subject: Inquiry about research opportunities - [Your Name], [Your Field]

Dear Professor [Last Name],

I am [Your Name], a [degree] graduate from [University], [Country].
I am interested in your research on [specific topic] after reading
your paper "[Title]".

I plan to apply for the MEXT Scholarship (Embassy Recommendation)
as a research student. I would like to ask if you are accepting
research students and if I may send my research plan and CV.

Thank you for your time.

Sincerely,
[Name]
[Email]
```

## 💡 Tips for a Strong Application

- **Start early.** Documents like transcripts and recommendation letters take time.
- **Follow the guidelines exactly.** Wrong paper size, missing signatures, or the wrong form can disqualify you.
- **Make your research plan specific.** State the problem, method, expected outcome, and why Japan.
- **Show how you will contribute** to your home country after returning.
- **Choose recommenders carefully.** Pick people who know your work well.
- **Study the exam format** using past questions.
- **Learn basic Japanese.** Even if your program is in English, it helps in selection and in daily life.
- **Keep copies** of everything you submit.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Sending documents directly to MEXT instead of the Embassy
- Missing the Embassy's deadline (they differ by country)
- Using outdated forms from previous years
- Weak or generic research plan
- Ignoring the age limit and birth-date cutoff
- Relying on unofficial blogs instead of the Embassy announcement
- Leaving the health certificate to the last minute

---

## 🚨 Scam Alert

- MEXT and Japanese Embassies **do not charge** any application or selection fee.
- Beware of agents promising "guaranteed MEXT" for money.
- Verify announcements only on official sites ending in `go.jp` (and the Embassy's own site).
- When in doubt, contact the Embassy directly.
